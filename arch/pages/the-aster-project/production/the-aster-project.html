The Aster Project

The Aster Project was developed in response to an urgent insurance crisis threatening services to foster youth across the State of California. In short, the Nonprofit Insurance Alliance (NIAC), which provides liability insurance to most of California’s FFAs, is no longer renewing policies. Without insurance, these agencies cannot operate, and the potential closure of FFAs could have devastating consequences. The Aster Project is designed to understand the impacts of the crisis in the rural North State and to develop sustainable, research-driven solutions to this and other significant systemic barriers impacting provision of child welfare and behavioral health services to youth in foster care

Why “Aster”

The Aster Project draws inspiration from the aster flower, a symbol of resilience and renewal. Just as the aster returns abundantly after a wildfire, we aim to promote growth and healing in our community's children and youth, encouraging them to thrive with renewed strength and hope in the face of adversity.

What is the Aster Project?

The Aster Project is a bold initiative aimed at advancing transformative solutions for services to youth in foster care in rural Northern California. Developed in response to the urgent foster agency insurance crisis, the project is designed to understand the impacts of this situation and to develop sustainable, research-driven solutions to this and other significant systemic barriers impacting provision of child welfare and behavioral health services to youth in foster care. The initial research and planning year will focus primarily on understanding challenges in five rural Northern California counties served by theMcConnell Foundation: Modoc, Shasta, Siskiyou, Tehama, and Trinity.

Who is involved?

The project is facilitated by Arch Collaborative in close collaboration with North StateTogether and Shasta County Office of Education. Arch Collaborative has a strong history of leading collaborative regional efforts, including our recent rural placement pilot. The project will also engage key partners in local government, education, community-based organizations, and other agencies.

Where did the funding come from?

This project is made possible through partnership with the McConnell Foundation.

What is the ultimate goal of the Project?

While addressing the FFA Insurance Crisis is the priority, ultimately the Aster Project aims to address the broader implications of statewide legislation on rural counties which impact the provision of critical services to foster youth. State legislation must be informed by on-the-ground realities to ensure sustainable outcomes for rural counties. Through data collection, research, and relational connectivity in the primary counties, the most significant challenges will be identified and become the focus for development of solutions.

What outcomes will result from the Project?

The initial year of this project will prioritize exploration of research-driven, tailored solutions resulting in the development of a regional strategic framework to ensure rural counties are equipped to adapt and thrive amidst state-wide changes and anticipated upcoming challenges impacting services to foster youth. At the end of this research and planning year, we will also develop a subsequent implementation plan if funding is made available.

What is the Foster Family Agency Insurance Crisis?

Currently, the foster care system in California is at a crossroads as insurance coverage has been disrupted for foster family agencies (FFAs) across the state. Due to legislation expanding liability for entities such as FFAs and resulting costly litigation trends, theNonprofits Insurance Alliance of California (NIAC)--the largest insurance provider of FFAs in California representing 90% of FFAs--is withdrawing all coverage for FFAs.

What is the impact of the crisis?

Many agencies are being forced to choose between closing their doors or paying exorbitant premiums that have increased by as much as four to eight times the prior costs. If unaddressed, this crisis will lead to widespread foster family losses, severely limiting safe and licensed placement options for foster youth. NIAC-insured entities represent an estimated 9,600 children in foster care across the state.

What solutions have been pursued?

The State of California has largely left it to the counties to identify and implement solutions to this issue. Some larger urban centers are offering increased rates to their FFA providers to support the increased insurance costs. Smaller rural counties lack the resources to provide this solution.

How will the Aster Project address this challenge?

The project will provide counties and state-wide stakeholders with data-driven insights to ensure that proposed solutions are regionally driven, united, and well-informed. We aim to accomplish this through data collection and analysis, coordinated communication, proactive planning and regional collaboration.

Why is it called "Aster Project"?

The Aster Project draws inspiration from the aster flower, a symbol of resilience and renewal. Just as the aster blooms after a wildfire, we aim to foster growth and healing in our community's children and youth, encouraging them to thrive with renewed strength and hope in the face of adversity.
